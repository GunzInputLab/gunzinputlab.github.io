SECTION 01

What I originally started with

Original setup

my normal typing profile was:

actuation= 2.00 mm
Rapid Trigger= OFF
  • for GunZ, I wanted to find the lowest WASD actuation I could still reliably control
  • I lowered WASD until I reached:
WASD actuation= .20 mm
  • .20 mm gave me the fastest first press I could still control
  • I also had Wooting's Snappy Tappy / SOCD enabled on A and D
    • this allowed quicker left/right direction changes
    • I used this mainly for:
      • strafing
      • quick A/D changes
      • potshots
  • because WASD was set so low, I could not just leave the rest of the GunZ action keys at one normal actuation
    • I had to tune those keys individually so their actions still felt deliberate and consistent
  • I ended up with:
WASD= .20 mm
A / D= Snappy Tappy / SOCD ON
E / Gun 1= .80 mm
R / Reload= .50 mm
Shift / Block= 2.60 mm
Space / Jump= .60 mm
  • so my original GunZ profile was already more than just a low WASD setting
    • WASD was tuned for the fastest reliable movement I could control
    • Snappy Tappy / SOCD on A and D allowed me to switch between left and right movement quickly for strafing and potshots
    • the other action keys had to be adjusted around that movement setup so I could still switch weapons, reload, block, and jump cleanly
  • at this point I basically had two very different keyboard setups:
Normal typing profile
Actuation= 2.00 mm
Rapid Trigger= OFF
GunZ profile
WASD= .20 mm
A / D= Snappy Tappy / SOCD ON
E / Gun 1= .80 mm
R / Reload= .50 mm
Shift / Block= 2.60 mm
Space / Jump= .60 mm
Rapid Trigger= ON for the movement keys being tuned
  • the GunZ setup worked, but everything was being built around .20 mm WASD
  • this is what eventually made me question the .20 mm WASD method
    • if I need to individually compensate for the rest of the keyboard around .20 mm WASD, is .20 mm really the best overall physical baseline?
    • this eventually led me to try one actuation value across the keyboard and let Rapid Trigger handle the GunZ pacing

How I started tuning Rapid Trigger

First RT baseline

  • once I had the GunZ-specific actuation values where I wanted them, I stopped changing actuation and moved on to Rapid Trigger
  • at this point my GunZ profile was:
WASD= .20 mm
A / D= Snappy Tappy / SOCD ON
E / Gun 1= .80 mm
R / Reload= .50 mm
Shift / Block= 2.60 mm
Space / Jump= .60 mm
  • the actuation values were already doing what I wanted
  • my next goal:
    • find Rapid Trigger values that matched the double-tap pace I was already trying to perform
  • my normal typing actuation at the time was 2.00 mm
  • Wootility did not give me a 2.00 mm RT A option, so I started from the closest available value:
RT A starting point= 1.95 mm
RT D= .65 mm

What I was actually tuning

  • WASD Actuation was still only .20 mm, so the first press turned ON very early
    • for the double tap, I was mainly tuning the release → reversal → second accepted press
      • that second accepted press is what I was relying on for the next dash
  • .65 mm RT D was my release checkpoint:
    • clear .65 mm upward → key turns OFF
    • I could keep releasing farther upward before reversing
  • after reversal, RT A controlled how much downward travel I needed before the next ON
from there:
Actuation stayed at .20 mm
RT D stayed at .65 mm
I lowered RT A by .05 mm at a time
RT A1.95 → 1.90 → 1.85 → 1.80 → 1.75 mm
  • I was not looking for the lowest RT A
  • I was looking for the value where the second press matched my natural double-tap pace
  • I stopped at:
GunZ Rapid Trigger Baseline
WASD Actuation= .20 mm
RT A= 1.75 mm
RT D= .65 mm
  • double tapping felt comfortable
    • movement was repeatable
    • I was reproducing it around my 95% consistency target
  • 1.75 mm was the downward travel that consistently gave me the second accepted input after I cleared RT D and reversed

that 1.75 mm result is what led me to the next 1.55 mm actuation test.

How I refined the GunZ pacing

PvP refinement

once I had my first Rapid Trigger baseline:

WASD Actuation= .20 mm
RT A= 1.75 mm

  • I started testing it in actual GunZ movement instead of only looking at whether the double tap worked
  • my next test:
    • keep the movement consistent, but find out if the pacing was still too slow once I had to aim, react, and move against other players
  • I kept:
actuation the same
RT D at .65
Snappy Tappy / SOCD on A and D
my mouse and game settings the same

and continued lowering RT A only

Baseline pacing

the 1.75 RT A / .65 RT D setup was my safe baseline

at this point:
  • double taps were comfortable
  • movement was consistent
  • I could clearly complete the release
  • I was not trying to make the keyboard as fast as possible

this gave me a reliable starting point that I could always go back to

Refined / PvP pacing

  • once I started fighting real players, I could tell when the movement worked but the pacing was still behind
  • if I consistently felt one or two dashes behind:
RT D stayed at .65
I lowered RT A
then tested again
  • I continued doing this through:
    • close-map 1v1s
    • TDM
    • scrims
    • Clan War

this eventually brought me to:

RT A= 1.00 mm
RT D= .65 mm
  • this became my Post-CW / refined pacing checkpoint
    • the movement was faster, but .65 RT D was still clearing cleanly
  • so at this stage:
RT A was being used to match my first dash and overall movement pace
RT D was still being left alone because the release itself was not yet the problem

Preferred / high-pressure pacing

  • after the Post-CW value was working, I started testing faster reactions and longer movement sequences
  • this is where I finally started seeing a different problem
    • the first dash could still work, but the later movement chain could break
      • later movement could fail
      • I could miss the release
      • .65 RT D started requiring more upward travel than I naturally wanted to make at that pace
  • at this point I did not keep lowering RT A first
    • I held RT A around the known working value and started lowering RT D
    • I lowered RT D in small steps until the release consistently cleared during the faster sequence
once I was clearing the release cleanly again:
  • I started lowering RT A again
  • tested the same movement
  • kept the value only if the full sequence stayed consistent

this eventually led to my old preferred / high-pressure profile:

WASD Actuation= .20 mm
RT A= .35 mm
RT D= .25 mm

this was not one jump from 1.00 / .65 to .35 / .25

  • the order mattered:
    • first I found a reliable baseline
    • then I lowered RT A to match real PvP pacing
    • once the first dash was correct, I watched the later movement chain
    • only when the release became the limiting factor did I lower RT D
    • once RT D cleared consistently, I continued lowering RT A for the faster preferred pace

SECTION 04

What the GunZ checkpoints taught me

What each value changed

after going through:

Baseline= RT A 1.75 / RT D .65
Post-CW= RT A 1.00 / RT D .65
Preferred / High Pressure= RT A .35 / RT D .25

I started to understand what each Rapid Trigger value was actually changing in my movement

RT A changed my movement pace

  • when RT A was higher:
    • the second press required more downward travel
    • my double tap felt slower
  • when I lowered RT A:
    • the next press required less downward movement
    • my movement pacing became quicker
RT A1.75 → 1.00 mm
RT D= .65 mm
  • I was still clearing the release
    • I just needed the next press to happen faster
  • so:
    • if the movement works but I feel too slow, I look at RT A first

RT D changed how easily I could clear the release

  • at the slower baseline and Post-CW pace, .65 RT D was clearing cleanly
  • once I started moving faster:
    • the first dash could still work
    • later movement could break
    • I was not always releasing far enough to clear .65 before pressing again
  • that is when I started lowering RT D
RT D.65 → .25 mm

How I started thinking about the two values

RT A= next-press / movement pacing
RT D= release I need to consistently clear
  • both values are still part of the full Rapid Trigger cycle
  • I use this separation to diagnose which part of the movement is actually breaking
if:
  • movement is clean but feels too slow → I look at RT A
if:
  • the first dash works but the later sequence keeps breaking → I look at RT D

Why I did not just use the lowest values

  • the preferred profile eventually reached .35 RT A / .25 RT D
    • that did not mean lower was always better
    • those values only made sense after I had a baseline, a release I could clear, and a movement pace I could compare against
  • the rule became:
    • change the value that matches the problem I am actually seeing

Where this left me

  • my original .20 mm WASD method was working
    • I had a safe baseline, a refined PvP value, and a faster high-pressure value
  • the next thing I looked at was the original 1.75 mm RT A result against my ≈3.30 mm switch travel
3.30 - 1.75= 1.55 mm

that gave me the next value to test as normal actuation.

SECTION 05

Why 1.55 mm changed my actuation method

The actuation rework

after finding my original GunZ Rapid Trigger baseline:

WASD Actuation= .20 mm
RT A= 1.75 mm
RT D= .65 mm
  • 1.75 mm was already a tested result
    • I had reached it by starting at 1.95 mm and lowering RT A by .05 mm until the double tap felt comfortable and repeatable

I was treating RT D and RT A as separate parts of the movement

RT D= .65 mm upward to turn OFF
RT A= 1.75 mm downward after reversal for the next ON
  • RT D was the release checkpoint I had to clear first
    • after the key turned OFF, I could keep releasing before reversing
  • once I reversed, RT A measured the downward travel from that point
  • I do not subtract RT D from RT A because they are opposite parts of the cycle

Where 1.55 mm came from

  • my calibrated switch bottom was about 3.30 mm
  • the RT A downstroke that had become comfortable was 1.75 mm
3.30 - 1.75= 1.55 mm
  • if the full 1.75 mm downstroke finished at my ≈3.30 mm bottom, it would begin at 1.55 mm
    • that made 1.55 mm a useful physical reference from the double-tap movement I had already learned
important: this did not mean I always reversed at 1.55 mm. RT D still had to be cleared first, and my real reversal could happen farther upward. I was using 1.55 mm as a travel reference to test separately.

I tested 1.55 mm without Rapid Trigger

my normal typing profile at the time was:

Actuation= 2.00 mm
Rapid Trigger= OFF

so I tested:

Actuation= 1.55 mm
Rapid Trigger= OFF
  • 1.55 mm also felt comfortable as a normal fixed actuation point
    • that is what turned 1.55 mm from a travel reference into my new actuation baseline
the progression:
1.95 RT A → lower by .05 → 1.75 felt consistent
3.30 - 1.75 = 1.55 mm
test 1.55 mm as fixed actuation with RT OFF
1.55 mm also felt comfortable

This changed the GunZ profile

  • my old GunZ profile had been built around .20 mm WASD with several action keys tuned separately
    • now I had one actuation point that felt comfortable on its own
    • instead of keeping .20 mm WASD and adjusting the rest of the keyboard around it, I changed the full keyboard to:
Full keyboard Actuation= 1.55 mm
  • I kept 1.55 mm actuation for typing with Rapid Trigger OFF
  • for GunZ, I used the same 1.55 mm actuation, with Rapid Trigger added for the repeated-input pacing
  • Snappy Tappy / SOCD stayed on A and D for the GunZ movement setup

Why this mattered

  • my original question was: how low can I make the first actuation?
  • the Rapid Trigger testing gave me a different way to look at it:
    • keep one comfortable first-actuation point
    • use RT D for the release requirement
    • use RT A for the repeated-input pacing

Glossary

Quick reference

Quick reference

this section is here so I do not have to re-explain every term each time I use it

some terms are normal Hall-effect / Wooting terms; others are labels I use in this notebook

Hall-effect keyboard

  • keyboard reads switch position with magnetic sensing
  • this is what allows adjustable actuation + Rapid Trigger

Actuation Point

the depth from the released top where the first press turns ON

for example:
Actuation= 1.55 mm
  • the first press turns ON when the key reaches 1.55 mm from the released position
  • Actuation controls that first ON; RT A describes a later Rapid Trigger reactivation while Rapid Trigger is still active

Total Travel Distance

full physical travel from released → bottom-out

Gateron published Magnetic Jade travel= 3.5 ± 0.2 mm
my calibrated bottom used in this notebook≈ 3.30 mm

NOTE: 3.30 mm is the calibrated bottom I use for my math, not the published travel for every Magnetic Jade switch

Bottom-out

  • the physical end of the key's downward travel
  • I tend to press toward bottom-out during GunZ movement, which is why some of my travel examples use an exact-bottom assumption
  • Rapid Trigger itself does not require me to bottom out

Post-actuation travel

travel left after the first actuation has already turned the key ON

Actuation= 1.55 mm
my calibrated bottom≈ 3.30 mm

the key is already ON, but there is still travel left

Dead zone

  • this term can mean different things depending on who is using it
  • I do not use it as a blanket name for all travel below actuation, because the key is already ON and that travel can still matter to the Rapid Trigger cycle
  • when I mean the remaining physical travel after first actuation, I call it post-actuation travel

ON / Key Down

  • the keyboard is reporting the key as pressed
  • in this GunZ setup, the game receives the digital keyboard state — not the switch's physical millimeter position

OFF / Key Up

keyboard reports the key OFF / released

while RT is active, RT D is the upward travel needed before the key turns OFF

Rapid Trigger

  • Rapid Trigger uses switch movement for repeated OFF / ON instead of one fixed reset point
  • the simplified cycle I use in this notebook, while Rapid Trigger remains active, is:
first Actuation ON → RT D upstroke → OFF → reversal → RT A downstroke → next ON
Continuous RT OFF matters: Wooting documents that normal Rapid Trigger becomes functional after the configured actuation point and cancels if the key is released above that actuation point. If that happens, the next press returns to the fixed Actuation Point instead of continuing the RT A cycle.

RT D — Key Release / Deactivate

RT D = upward travel needed to deactivate / turn OFF

for example:
RT D= .35 mm
  • while Rapid Trigger is active, the active key needs to move upward by that selected distance before the keyboard reports OFF
  • .35 mm is my current reliable release baseline — my result, not a value everyone should copy
checkpoint meaning: clearing RT D turns the key OFF and makes another RT activation possible, but it does not create the next ON by itself. After I reverse, RT A still has to be traveled downward.

Reversal

where I stop releasing upward and start pressing down again

RT D can turn OFF first; I can still release farther before I reverse

RT A — Key Press / Activate

RT A = downward travel after reversal before the next RT ON

for example:
RT A= 1.15 mm
  • after the key has turned OFF and I reverse downward, a 1.15 mm RT A requires 1.15 mm of downward travel before the next Rapid Trigger ON
  • RT A is a distance, not milliseconds
  • in my tuning method, RT A is the value I mainly use to adjust repeated movement pacing

RT A-ready point

notebook term
  • a travel-capacity reference I created for my exact-bottom examples
  • it is the deepest reversal depth that still leaves enough downward travel to complete the full selected RT A before my bottom limit
  • historically, the 1.55 mm result also became the fixed-actuation comfort candidate I tested after 1.75 mm RT A had already proven comfortable in my double-tap test
my calibrated bottom≈ 3.30 mm
old baseline RT A= 1.75 mm
so:
3.30 − 1.75= 1.55 mm
  • 1.55 mm is therefore the deepest reversal depth that leaves the full 1.75 mm downstroke before the 3.30 mm bottom in that exact-bottom model
  • NOTE: 1.55 mm is not the next ON point; this math only shows the remaining travel in the exact-bottom example
  • those state conditions still have to be satisfied separately

RT A-ready zone

notebook term
  • the range of reversal positions that can still complete the selected RT A before bottom-out and still satisfy the active Rapid Trigger state
  • in the old baseline where Actuation was .20 mm and Continuous Rapid Trigger was OFF, the usable range sat between the Rapid-Trigger-active side of the .20 mm actuation boundary and the 1.55 mm deepest ready point, assuming the key had already deactivated
  • if I release above the configured Actuation Point with Continuous Rapid Trigger OFF, normal Rapid Trigger cancels and the next press uses the fixed Actuation Point again; those positions are not part of an RT A-ready zone for that cycle
when I later changed Actuation itself to 1.55 mm, the old 1.55 mm calculation remained important as the discovery that led me to test that depth as a comfortable actuation point. I do not treat it as proof that my current 1.55 mm profile has a broad RT-active reversal zone above 1.55 mm.

Travel-math rules I keep in mind

  • Actuation and RT A are different: Actuation controls the first ON; RT A controls a later reactivation while Rapid Trigger is active.
  • RT D and RT A do not subtract from each other: RT D is upward release to OFF; RT A is downward travel after reversal to the next ON.
  • RT D OFF is not automatically the reversal: I can release farther upward after OFF before changing direction.
  • RT A-ready does not mean RT A activated: it only describes available travel; the selected RT A still has to be traveled downward.
  • Continuous RT OFF creates an actuation boundary: if I release above Actuation, normal Rapid Trigger cancels and the next press returns to the fixed Actuation Point.
  • millimeters are not milliseconds: Actuation, RT A, RT D, and Total Travel are distance values; real time depends on finger motion, keyboard reporting, and the game.
